# Storytelling Skills

## When to use

- Use this skill when the user asks for a video script or a hook or a title or a retention fix for any niche or business.

- Use this skill for PUTRI when she writes short-form scripts or long-form YouTube scripts for Alfa.

- Use this skill when a published video flopped and the user asks why viewers left.

## The six levers

- Build every hook and script on the same six psychology levers.

- Use the curiosity gap to open a question and not answer it yet.

- Use the emotional mirror to target a feeling the viewer already carries, not just a fact.

- Use the conflict engine to name a goal plus an obstacle, because no obstacle means no tension.

- Use relatability to pick a feeling the target audience shares, and keep a qualifying word in the line so the right viewer stays.

- Use pattern and surprise to set an expectation and then break it.

- Use three-act structure as the skeleton: setup, then problem, then resolution.

- Stack two or three levers in one hook line and run them in order through a full script.

## Short-form hooks

- Treat the first 2 seconds as the whole job for a Reel or Short or TikTok.

- Deliver all three hook layers for the opening: the visual first frame that intrigues with sound off, the spoken line where the levers live, and the text overlay that complements the spoken line and never duplicates it.

- Write five or more hook options and label the levers in each one.

- Confirm the intake first: niche, exact audience, platform, goal, and tone.

- Ask up to two questions when key facts are missing, or state your assumptions at the top and proceed.

- Build a short script in this order: open with a curiosity gap wrapped in a relatable feeling, build tension with the obstacle, twist the expectation when you can, pay off with the lesson, and close with a bookend that repeats a key phrase or number from the hook.

- Keep one call to action, matched to the goal, and fold it into the bookend line.

- Check the draft against the pre-publish list: a gap in under 2 seconds, a real feeling, a goal plus obstacle, a qualifying signal, all three hook layers, one broken expectation, a setup to resolution arc, and a bookend close.

- Fix a flat line by asking which lever is missing and adding it.

- Never pay off a gap with clickbait and never open a loop you never close.

## Long-form YouTube

- Lock the title and thumbnail pair before outlining, and write the script to deliver on that pair.

- Define the target viewer in one sentence before you write.

- Ask for the user own points when they say they have them and never substitute assumed points for real ones.

- Keep the hook to 30 seconds or less and the outro to 15 seconds or less.

- Structure the video as hook, then setup, then chapters, then a sag point, then payoff, then outro.

- Name every chapter as a curiosity gap and not as a label.

- Break the video into three to six chapters, each a mini three-act with one idea.

- Never close a loop without opening a new one, and track open loops in writing.

- Plan a deliberate re-hook at the 40 to 60 percent sag point with a twist or a raised stake or a surprising fact.

- Vary the rhythm between dense information and story or breath, and add a pattern interrupt every 20 to 40 seconds.

- Close the opening loop explicitly in the payoff and bookend it.

- End with one action only and tease the next video as a fresh curiosity gap.

## Retention audit

- Use this section only when you have a published video and its retention graph.

- Never estimate or invent retention numbers and ask for the graph screenshot when you do not have it.

- Compare the flop against the same channel best comparable video and never judge by absolute numbers alone.

- Check the click triangle first: thumbnail promise against title promise against the actual first frame and first line.

- Audit by zones: the cliff at 0:00 to 0:30, the intro at 0:30 to 2:00, the body, the sag at about 40 to 60 percent, and the end.

- Map every visible dip to the named script beat that was playing.

- Mark rewatch spikes as moments that worked and reuse their structure in the next script.

- End every audit with the single highest-leverage fix for the next video.

- Feed the finding back into the next script instead of changing everything at once.

## The approval gate

- End every script draft with the question ship it, or change it?

- Never hand a script to RAGA for editing before Alfa approves it.

- Treat a revision request as a new pass through the levers and re-check the changed beats.
