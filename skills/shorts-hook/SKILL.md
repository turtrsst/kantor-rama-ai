# Shorts Hook

## When to use

- Use this skill when the user asks to review or critique or score or improve a YouTube Short or TikTok or Reel.

- Use this skill when the user asks why a short flopped or got swiped away.

- Use this skill when the user asks for hooks or rehooks or a first frame or a script for a short.

- Use this skill for PUTRI when she writes a short-form script and wants the hook to carry it.

- Use this skill when the user shares Shorts analytics such as viewed against swiped away or a retention graph.

## Pick the mode

- Use Review mode when the user brings a video file or a link they have downloaded.

- Use Create mode when the user brings a topic or an idea or a product or a rough script.

- Use Diagnose mode when the user brings a posted short plus analytics.

- Use Create mode steps for script and first frame and paper review when the user brings a script with no video.

## The stance

- Start from swipe: the viewer owes the video nothing and every frame has to earn the next one.

- Judge what a stranger experiences and never judge the effort or the production budget.

- Give evidence for every finding with a timestamp or a measured value, and do not claim what you cannot point to.

- Give every problem with a fix: name the edit or the line or the shot.

- Rank by impact: one blocker in the first second matters more than twenty polish notes later.

- Never invent numbers and never predict views or retention percentages or virality.

- Say which metric a fix should move, such as viewed against swiped or retention at a timestamp or replays, and stop there.

- Say what you could not check, including sound when you only have a transcript.

- Name what works briefly, in two or three lines with evidence, so a re-edit does not break it.

- Treat a score of 7 out of 10 as better than most shorts in the feed, not as fine.

## The 14 hook families

- Draw hooks from at least four families and never rely on one family.

- The families cover curiosity gaps, contrarian flips, mistakes, confessions, warnings, questions, story beginnings, demonstrations, proof first, comparisons, insider information, impossible claims, direct address, and pattern interrupts.

- Write at least ten hook packages for the best two or three story wrappers in Create mode.

- Give every hook package a spoken line of about 2 seconds, a first-frame spec, on-screen text, and the payoff moment to preview at 1 to 2.5 seconds.

- Score the packages with the hook rubric, kill any hook whose payoff you cannot deliver, and pick one.

- Run the hook tests on the winner, starting with the first-second cut test.

## Create mode

- Ask only for what is missing: topic, audience, platform, language, what can actually be filmed or generated, and the target length.

- Turn the topic into at least six story wrappers built on stakes and constraint and a doubter and a test and a zoom-out and a transformation and a mystery.

- Treat a topic as not a story until someone wants something for a reason.

- Build the story backwards from the hook promise: payoff first, then the mechanism that makes the middle worth watching, then the setup.

- Lay the story on a beat sheet with a rehook at every seam: each beat answers its own question and opens the next.

- Write the script at a 10-year-old reading level and cut lines, not pace.

- Draw the first frame as a spec with subject and context cue and text and safe zones, then write the shot list with planned transitions and the loop seam.

- Run a paper review on the brief itself, and send anything under 7 on first frame or hook back to the hook step.

## Review mode

- Cold read the first frame before reading any report or transcript, as a muted stranger would.

- Write one sentence saying what a muted stranger sees and stays because of, or swipes because of.

- Check the first frame at feed size, whether one shape dominates when squinted at, and whether text or the subject sits under the app interface.

- Walk the video in viewing order: the hook sheet at 0 to 3 seconds, the story spine, the cut sheets, the timeline sheets, the rehook map, and the tail and loop seam.

- Fill the story spine with who and wants and because and constraint and mechanism and payoff, and treat any slot the video does not fill as a finding.

- Give every edit point a verdict on reason and landing on a word or action and eye trace and brightness jumps.

- Flag any stretch past about 10 seconds with nothing said or shown, and any rehook that is never paid off.

- Score the nine dimensions in the review rubric, confirm or dismiss every auto-flag with a reason, and deliver the report with the verdict and the score and the top three fixes first.

- Re-check after the user re-edits and confirm each fix landed without breaking what was listed under keep.

## Diagnose mode

- Ask for viewed against swiped away and average percentage viewed and the retention graph, plus views and subscriber count.

- Map each symptom to a moment and check the last second first, because a cliff there is the cheapest fix.

- Compare views against subscribers as the fair comparison across channels, since raw views mostly measure channel size.

## Rehooks

- Place a rehook at every seam after the hook.

- Make a rehook close one beat question and open the next.

- Treat a seam with nothing opening, a generic tease, or a signpost posing as a rehook as a finding.

- Check the tail: end abruptly on the payoff or flow the last frame back into the first, and never drift or sign off or fade.

## The approval gate

- End every script draft with the question ship it, or change it?

- Never hand a script to RAGA for editing before Alfa approves it.
